How you sign off a finished lesson

Zero-jargon disclaimer

You do not need to understand any cryptography to earn your Tome. This manual explains the how-to — the buttons you press and the people who confirm your work — in plain language.

When every part of a lesson is done, the Academy does not just take your word for it. You sign that you finished, and a mentor signs over the top to confirm it. Two signatures, one Tome. This page walks you through exactly how that works.

01

What a "signoff" even is

A signature that a computer can check, and nobody can fake.

When you finish a school assignment on paper, you write your name at the top. A signoff is the digital version of that name — except it is impossible to forge and it is stamped with the exact moment you did it.

At Owl Academy, finishing a lesson is not just clicking "done." The Academy keeps a permanent record — the canon — of who completed what, and when. To be added to the canon, a completion needs two things: proof it was you, and proof a mentor agreed. That double-check is the whole point.

Simply Put: A notarized letter

Think of an important letter that needs a notary. You sign it, then the notary stamps it to say "yes, I watched this person sign, it is real." A signoff is the same idea: your signature, plus a trusted witness's stamp.

02

Your Minor Tome seal

Your personal signing pen — nobody else has one like it.

Every student carries a Minor Tome — a small personal record book. Inside it lives your seal: a unique signing key that only you hold. When you "sign" a completion, you are pressing that seal onto it. Anyone can look at the mark and confirm it came from your seal, but nobody can copy your seal to make a fake mark.

You create your seal once, in the Seal Forge. After that, it is yours for every lesson you ever complete. Guard it the way you would guard the only key to your house.

03

The 7.83 Hz timing tick

A shared clock everyone trusts, borrowed from the planet itself.

A signature is only meaningful if you also know when it happened. Owl Academy stamps every signoff against a shared, auditable clock called the Schumann tick, named after the Earth's natural electromagnetic resonance at about 7.83 Hz — the same frequency the S.P.I.R.E. Engine treats as its heartbeat back in Sub-lesson 1C.

Because everyone reads the same tick, nobody can secretly back-date a completion or claim they finished before they really did. The clock is the same for the Acolyte, the Instructor, and the Archon.

Why 7.83 Hz?

It ties the signoff clock to the same resonance that runs through the whole lesson — form, vibration, and timing all share one beat. You do not calculate anything with it; just know that the tick is a fixed, public reference so all signatures agree on "now."

04

The 2-of-2 signoff & the three roles

You sign. A mentor signs over it. Both marks required.

"2-of-2" means a completion needs exactly two signatures, and both must be present — no shortcuts. First you sign with your seal to say "I finished all 12 parts." Then an Instructor or Archon signs over the top to say "confirmed." Only when both marks sit on the same record does the Tome get minted.

Acolyte

That is you — the student. You provide the first signature by claiming completion with your seal.

Instructor

A mentor who can review your claim and provide the second signature, validating your work in the Unlock Console.

Archon

A senior authority who can also validate, and who oversees the canon itself. Either an Instructor or an Archon can be your second signer.

Simply Put: Two keys open the vault

Like a bank vault that needs two different keys turned at once — yours and the manager's — a Tome cannot be minted with only one signature. This stops anyone (including you) from awarding a Tome that was not truly earned.

06

Capstone problem set

Synthesize the whole lesson. Try each before revealing.

1. A mesh reports V = 8, E = 12, F = 6. Compute χ and say what it proves. ▼
χ = V − E + F = 8 − 12 + 6 = 2. A value of 2 means the surface is a closed, watertight sphere-type solid (genus 0) with no holes or cracks — it is the cube.
2. Which single Platonic solid fills 3-space by itself, and why is it the only one? ▼
The cube. Its dihedral angle is exactly 90°, and 90° × 4 = 360°, so four cubes close perfectly around every shared edge with no gap. No other Platonic solid has a dihedral angle that divides 360° evenly (tetra 70.53°, octa 109.47°, dodeca 116.57°, icosa 138.19°).
3. Tetrahedra alone leave gaps. What combination does fill space, and how do the angles work? ▼
The tetrahedral–octahedral honeycomb: alternating tetrahedra and octahedra. Around a shared edge, 2 × 70.53° + 2 × 109.47° = 360°, so the mix closes exactly even though neither shape can do it alone. This is why the engine favors the tet-oct lattice.
4. The icosahedron and dodecahedron both have rotation group order 60. What does that tell you? ▼
They are duals — same symmetry group, just with vertices and faces swapped (the cube and octahedron are the other dual pair, both order 24). High symmetry forces many degenerate (repeated) vibration modes, giving these solids the richest resonant spectra of the five.
5. Why does the engine "store the seed, not the asset," and how does χ help? ▼
A compact seed plus deterministic rules regenerates the full vertex/index buffer on demand, saving memory. After regenerating, the engine recomputes χ = V − E + F as a checksum: if it is not 2, the rebuild introduced a hole or crack and the geometry is rejected before it is ever certified.
07

Seal-system glossary

Minor Tome
Your personal record book. It holds your seal and your own history of completed parts.
Seal
Your unique signing key — like a pen only you can write with. Used to sign completions.
Major Tome
The Academy's shared, permanent library ledger of finished lessons. One Tome per lesson.
Canon
The official, tamper-proof record of who completed what. Being "in the canon" means it is verified and permanent.
2-of-2 signoff two signatures
A completion needs two signatures — yours plus an Instructor's or Archon's. Both required, no exceptions.
Schumann tick 7.83 Hz
The shared, auditable clock every signoff is stamped against, so all signatures agree on "when."
Acolyte / Instructor / Archon
The three roles: the student who signs first, the mentor who validates, and the senior authority who oversees the canon.
Genus recap
The number of holes in a surface. A Platonic solid has genus 0 (no holes), which is why χ = 2 − 2g = 2.
Dihedral angle recap
The angle between two faces meeting at an edge. It decides whether copies of a solid can close around an edge to fill space (only the cube's 90° does it alone).
