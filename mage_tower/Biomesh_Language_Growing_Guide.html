01 — Overview

What Is Language Growing?

BioChain Language Growing (formerly "Biomesh Mind Eye") is the page where a grower turns their own recorded biochains into a retrieval familiar — a memory that recalls the grower's own real, previously-written language instead of generating text a model hasn't learned to speak. It is built on the same order-2 rank coder and SHD-CCP packet architecture as the rest of the Biomesh system, and shares its engram-crystallization code, character-for-character, with Library/Experimental_Systems/Engram_Mind_Eye.html.

The page exists to answer one honest question head-on: if the underlying model can't reliably generate legible language, how do you get useful responses out of it? The answer here is retrieval, not generation — recall the grower's own passages by Hamming-nearest engram match — with two optional, clearly-labeled escape hatches (BART, or a bring-your-own external API) for when synthesis over the recalled context is actually wanted. Every single turn, on every engine, is logged to a tamper-evident ledger, so the entire session is a replayable, auditable chain of thought.

🔏

Seal

Every turn can be signed by an operator's minor-tome seal, rendered as a unique SHD-CCP familiar sigil — cryptographic authorship, not just an internal log entry.

🌱

Grow

Your biochains are split into passages and crystallized into engram keys — the corpus the familiar retrieves from is language you actually wrote.

📜

Audit

Every turn is hashed, folded into a running parity, optionally signed, and replayable — a full chain of thought that can be exported, crystallized, or verified.

Archon-only surface

Language Growing is gated the same way as the Genesis Forge and the Biomesh Console — the page resolves your tier client-side via resolveTier() and only renders for ARCHON, with Firestore rules as the write backstop. Everyone can still grow, transfer, and rate biochains elsewhere in the Biomesh — this particular language-seed surface is Archon-scoped.

02 — Seals

Operator Seals

An operator seal is a minor-tome ECDSA P-256 keypair (see scripts/minor-tome.js) that you select at the top of the Language Growing page. Once selected, every turn you generate is signed with a COT/1|index|contentHash|parity payload — Chain-Of-Thought, version 1 — so the log isn't just internally consistent, it's attributable to a specific, publicly verifiable identity.

Each seal renders as its own unique SHD-CCP sigil — the same familiar-rendering engine used for cosmological IDs elsewhere in the Academy (renderUserSigil), but folded from the seal's own sealVector hex into a manifold (sealManifoldFromVector → n/k/t lattice parameters). Mint two seals and you get two visually distinct familiars — deterministic, so the same seal always renders the same sigil, but unique per seal. 

// scripts/sigil-renderer.js
sealManifoldFromVector(sealVector)  // sealVector hex → { n, k, t }
renderSealSigil(seal, { size })     // seal → SVG, keyed by sealId

Minting a Seal

This is the same walkthrough behind the "?" help button on the Language Growing page itself:

1

Open the Seal Forge

Visit Seal_Forge.html in a new tab — you don't need to leave your growing session to do this.

2

Adjust the lattice, if you want

Manifold, coherency, and torsion controls affect the sigil's geometry and the seal's derivation inputs. The defaults are fine for a first seal.

3

Click Forge minor tome

This samples the current Schumann tick as entropy and mints an ECDSA P-256 keypair. The private key never leaves your browser — only the public seal record is written to your Firestore seals/ document.

4

Return to Language Growing

Your new seal appears in the Operator Seal selector automatically. Reload the page if it doesn't show up right away.

5

Select it

Its sigil renders immediately next to the selector, and every turn from that point on is signed COT/1 under that seal.

Recall as a Kill Switch

Seals can be recalled from the Seal Forge. A recalled seal renders with a red broken-ring-and-strike overlay everywhere its sigil appears, and instantly invalidates every signature it ever produced — including signatures already baked into an exported engram file or a crystallized biochain. This mirrors the Tome Seal System's "send out, then recall validity" model: you can revoke trust in a compromised or retired identity without needing to track down and delete every artifact it ever signed.

⚠ Recall is global and permanent

Recalling a seal is not scoped to one session or one biochain — it invalidates that seal's authority everywhere, for good. Only recall a seal you actually intend to retire.

03 — Sources

Data Sources

The left panel's data source selector controls what the familiar can recall from. There are two modes:

SourceCorpusWhen to use it
STRIX default Built-in, fixed concept library with hand-written responses No biochain loaded yet, or you want the familiar's original hard-coded persona
Biochain seed Your own recreated biochain, split into passages by biochainToSeeds() You want the familiar to recall your real writing, not a fixed script

Loading a biochain seed recreates the chain losslessly (proving you actually hold the body, not just its hashes), splits the text into passages, and crystallizes each passage into an engram key with engramOf/engramGrid — the identical XOR crystallization used in Engram_Mind_Eye.html. A query you type is crystallized the same way, then matched to the nearest passage by Hamming distance (matchSeeds()).

Honest limit

XOR-of-letters engrams are a coarse bag-of-letters key — strong for near-duplicate recall, weak for semantic similarity (measured Hamming distances cluster near 32/64, i.e. close to random, for genuinely different meanings). Pure retrieval is best-effort recall; it gets better as you accumulate more, better-segmented biochains, not from tuning a model.

04 — Engines

Response Engines

Once a query is matched to recalled passages, the engine setting decides what happens next:

None — pure retrieval

reproducible

Returns the nearest recalled passage(s) verbatim. Free, fully local, no network call. Legible by construction — it's real text a human wrote. Because retrieval is a pure function of the query and the loaded seeds, re-running the same query against the same corpus always yields the same result: bit-for-bit re-derivable on replay.

BART — in-browser synthesis

attested

Loads Xenova/bert-base-uncased (quantized) via Transformers.js from a CDN and runs masked-language-model fill-mask synthesis over the recalled context, entirely client-side — no API key, no server. Because a masked-LM sample is not guaranteed to be identical every time, it is always logged as attested, never reproducible: the turn is faithfully recorded and hash-chained, but not provably re-derivable byte-for-byte.

API — bring your own AI

attested

The top-K recalled passages are injected as {{context}} and your query as {{query}} into a fully configurable HTTP request — endpoint, headers, body template, and a response dot-path to extract the answer, with presets for OpenAI, Anthropic, and Ollama. This is retrieval-augmented generation with your biochain as the corpus. Your API key stays in the browser and is never written to Firestore. Also always logged as attested.

Reproducible vs. Attested

This is the honesty framework the whole page is built around, and it's never blurred: a reproducible turn can be bit-for-bit re-derived by replaying the same query against the same corpus — the ledger's Verify replay action actually recomputes it and checks the hash. An attested turn (BART or API) is faithfully recorded and hash-chained — you can prove exactly what was asked, what context was supplied, and what came back, and that the record hasn't been tampered with — but you cannot prove the model would say the same thing twice. Every turn in the ledger is tagged with which kind it is, so a reviewer auditing the chain of thought always knows which guarantee applies to which step.

05 — Quarantine

The Quarantine Zone

The query input field is explicitly treated as a quarantine zone: whatever you type is crystallized and used to match against recalled passages — it is never executed, evaluated, or interpolated into code. On the API engine, your query only ever reaches the template substitution as an inert string ({{query}}), the same discipline the rest of the Biomesh system applies to any user-supplied text. This keeps the page safe to point at biochains you didn't write yourself, and keeps the honesty framework intact — the model doesn't get to act on your input, only respond to it.

06 — Ledger

The Audit Ledger

Every turn — on any engine — is appended to the in-page audit ledger and hash-chained into a running sessionParity, seeded to 0x53484443 ("SHDC"): 

contentHash = SHA-256(query + engine + class + context + response)
sessionParity = fold32Hex(contentHash) XOR rotl32(sessionParity, 1)

This is the identical fold/rotate/XOR lineage pattern as Engram_Mind_Eye.html's codexParity/packetChain — a change to any turn, anywhere in the session, changes every parity value computed after it. If an operator seal is selected, each turn is additionally signed: 

COT/1 | index | contentHash | parity     ← signed with signWithMinorTome()

So the ledger gives you two independent layers of tamper evidence: the hash chain (detects any edit to the log itself) and the seal signature (attributes each turn to a specific, publicly verifiable identity). Both are checked separately by Verify replay.

Export · Crystallize · Replay

Export engram

Downloads the full session log — every turn, hash, and signature — as a local JSON file. Nothing leaves your machine.

Crystallize session

Publishes the session as a biochain via publishBiochain, titled "Language Growing session " + timestamp — turns the chain of thought itself into a tradable, certified artifact.

Verify replay

Re-walks the ledger from turn 1, recomputing every contentHash and refolding sessionParity from scratch. Any turn whose recorded hash doesn't match what's recomputed is flagged — independent of the seal signatures.

07 — Burn

Burning a Session

🔥 Burn session destructively clears the current, local, unexported working session — its content is not recoverable afterward. This is a deliberate reset for when you want a clean growing session, not an accident-recovery feature.

Burning content sounds like it's at odds with "everything must be logged" — it isn't, because burning does not erase the fact that a burn happened. logSessionBurn(uid, turnCount, parityHex) writes a biomesh.session_burned event to the append-only chronicles collection, recording who burned, how many turns were destroyed, and the session's final parity hash — never the turn content itself. So the system stays auditable at the "a burn happened, by whom, how much was destroyed" level, while still giving you a genuine way to discard content you don't want kept.

The confirm prompt is context-aware

If your session has already been exported or crystallized, the confirm dialog simply confirms the burn (a saved copy exists elsewhere). If there are unsaved turns, the warning is explicit: "This session has N UNSAVED turn(s). Burning now destroys them permanently — export or crystallize first if you want to keep them."

There is no undo

Once burned, the local session log, its ledger view, and its parity chain are all reset to empty. If you didn't export or crystallize first, that content is gone — only the meta-fact of the burn survives, in chronicles.

08 — Workflow

Full Workflow

Path A — Growing your first language seed

1

Grow and publish a biochain

Use the Biomesh Console's Grow tab to grow a chain from real text you've written, then certify and publish it.

2

Open Language Growing and load it as a seed

Select "Biochain seed" as the data source and pick your published chain. It recreates losslessly and splits into passages.

3

Mint and select an operator seal

Follow Path B (or the "?" button) if you don't have one yet — this signs every turn from here on.

4

Query, review, iterate

Type a query, review the recalled passage(s), switch engines if you want synthesis instead of raw retrieval. Watch the ledger grow.

5

Export, crystallize, or burn

When you're done, export the engram, crystallize the session as a new biochain, or burn it if it was just exploratory.

Path B — Minting your first seal

1

Open the Seal Forge and click "Forge minor tome"

2

Return to Language Growing and pick it from the selector

3

Confirm its sigil renders, then continue growing — turns now sign as COT/1
